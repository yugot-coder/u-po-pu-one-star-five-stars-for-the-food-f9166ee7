# Candidates — One Star, Five Stars for the Food

All candidates are one sentence (exactly one period — the final one) and
inside the 200-character gate. Direction: a specific, visual alien grievance
(gravity, queues, glowing rectangles, small talk, weather) + a concrete,
unexpected food compliment — never generic ("humans are weird", "traffic bad").

## 1. ✅ CHOSEN — 165 characters

> One star: Earth's gravity dragged my third eye shut by noon and the locals queue politely for misery, but the ramen broth alone was worth the nine-light-year flight.

Why it wins: the most alien-specific complaint of the set (gravity + a third
eye), a second outsider observation (humans queue politely for misery), and a
food compliment that is concrete (ramen broth) with the funniest turn — the
broth justified a nine-light-year flight, yet the review stays one star.
Deadpan, fresh, every word earns its place.

## 2. — 172 characters

> One star: every human stared at a glowing rectangle instead of a perfectly good sunset while the rain fell sideways, but the fried chicken tasted like a small captured sun.

Strong image (glowing rectangles vs. sunset; sideways rain) and a vivid food
line, but two complaints dilute the single grievance and "small captured sun"
is prettier than it is funny.

## 3. — 178 characters

> One star: strangers kept asking how I was without wanting an answer and the weather changed its mind hourly, but the tacos — crisp, bright, defiant — nearly earned a second star.

Great outsider bafflement (small talk) and a punchy food triplet, but
"nearly earned a second star" softens the one-star stance — the review
almost stops being a one-star review.

## 4. — 168 characters

> One star: I queued forty minutes for a ride that lasted ninety seconds while humans apologized to a machine, yet the street dumplings made me forgive the entire planet.

Specific numbers make the queue grievance land, and "forgive the entire
planet" is a big turn — but "forgive" concedes too much; the winner keeps the
grudge while praising the broth.

## 5. — 174 characters

> One star: I waited an hour for a table while humans photographed their food instead of eating it, but the satay arrived smoky and perfect, and I suddenly understood the cult.

Excellent outsider observation (photographing food) and concrete satay praise;
"understood the cult" is funny, but the sentence has three beats and the turn
arrives late — the winner's single "but" pivot is sharper.

## 6. — 172 characters

> One star: Earth's sky leaked without warning and its beds were clearly designed by an enemy species, but the breakfast pancakes were warm clouds wearing syrup, so I stayed.

"Sky leaked" and "beds designed by an enemy species" are fresh and alien,
but "so I stayed" resolves the joke into cosiness instead of keeping the
one-star deadpan.

## 7. — 166 characters

> One star: humans kept saying 'enjoy your meal' and then watching me eat, which is surveillance, but the melted cheese on everything almost made the spying forgivable.

The surveillance framing of a polite phrase is genuinely alien and funny;
"melted cheese on everything" is a bit generic as the food compliment, and
"almost made the spying forgivable" hedges where the winner commits.

## 8. — 154 characters

> One star: Earth's gravity flattened my luggage, my posture, and my will to live by Tuesday, but the pizza crust — charred, chewy, defiant — kept me alive.

Tightest escalation of the set (luggage → posture → will to live) and a
great crust triplet; the runner-up. It loses to #1 only because "kept me
alive" is a smaller turn than a nine-light-year flight justified by broth.

## 9. — 154 characters

> One star: the sun here is a rumor, the rain is a lifestyle, and strangers smile at nothing, but the flat white was so good I briefly considered defecting.

"Sun is a rumor" is a lovely alien line and "considered defecting" is a
strong closer, but three mini-complaints in a row read as a list, and a flat
white is a thinner food compliment than ramen broth or satay.

## 10. — 166 characters

> One star: I landed in weather that could not decide and stood behind humans arguing with a self-checkout, but the noodles apologized for the whole planet in one bite.

"Arguing with a self-checkout" is sharp outsider bafflement and the noodles
apologizing is a charming turn — but "weather that could not decide" is the
most generic complaint in the set, and the food line personifies the noodles
instead of praising their taste.

## Final choice

Candidate 1. It is the only one where complaint, alien voice, food praise,
and the turn all peak at once: a body-horror gravity grievance only an alien
would file, a queue observation only an outsider would make, ramen broth as
the concrete reward, and a punchline that makes the interstellar trip worth
it — while the star stays one.
